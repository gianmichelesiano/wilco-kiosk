## Operations

Some operations need a short explanation.

| Operation | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Big bag** | There is no separate operation called Big bag. These flights are flown under **HESLO 2**. |
| **D-Bag** | The paraglider is packed in a bag. The paraglider pilot jumps out of the helicopter, and the task specialist (TSOP) holds the bag so that the paraglider opens cleanly. |
| **Firefighting** | Flown as a state flight. You find it under the positions **State Flight Disaster** and **TSOP State Flight Disaster**. State flights are not regulated by EASA, so this operation is not part of the EASA rules. |
| **H46** | H46 is not an operation. It is the type designator of the Boeing Vertol 107 (BV-107), a heavy helicopter. In Switzerland, for example, it is used for heavy load flights. For this type there are the positions **SPO Pilot H46** and **SPO Co-Pilot H46**, and operations with H46 in their name, for example *HESLO 2 Long Line H46*. **CP** at the end of an operation name stands for the co-pilot, for example *HESLO 1 H46 CP*. |

## Pilot in command, Copilot and PICUS

**Why is there no PIC role?** Every helicopter pilot who flies alone is the pilot in command (PIC). That is why NGFT Crew has no separate role for it. The role **Pilot** is the pilot in command.

**Copilot.** The second pilot in the cockpit gets the role **Copilot**.

**PICUS** (pilot in command under supervision). Use the settings of the aircraft operation: switch on **Under Supervision** and add **Limitations**, for example for visibility or wind. You set both on the member's page ([chapter 12](12-member-edit.md)). The dots in the Aircraft Overview show them ([chapter 13](13-overviews.md)).

## Personal documents

You can track personal documents of your crew, for example an ID, a personal data sheet or a psychological assessment, in the Relations Tree.

NGFT provides two of them for all companies: **ID - Passport** and **Personal Data Sheet**. Check first whether the one you need is among them:

1. Open the **Relations Tree** ([chapter 17](17-relations-tree.md)) and choose the role (for example Pilot).
2. Search under **Courses / Certificates** and add the document to the tree.
3. If you cannot find it, **search again with other words or ask NGFT (support@ngft.com) before you create your own**. The same document should exist only once, so please avoid duplicates.
4. Only if it really does not exist, create a custom certificate ([chapter 15](15-company-courses.md)) and add it to the tree (see **Add your own certificate** in [chapter 17](17-relations-tree.md)).

### Psychological assessment

There is no standard certificate for the psychological assessment, because the rule depends on the pilot's company. A pilot who **stays in the same company** does not have to repeat it. A pilot who **changes company** has to do it again if the last one is **more than 2 years ago**. Create your own custom certificate (step 4) and set the validity period that applies in your company.

## ESET and First Aid for TSOP

EASA does not require proof of ESET (Emergency and Safety Equipment) or First Aid training for TSOPs. The NGFT OM therefore does not describe them, and the Relations Tree does not list them as required documents for the TSOP.

If your company trains its TSOPs anyway and issues certificates, you can add them to the tree:

1. Open the **Relations Tree** ([chapter 17](17-relations-tree.md)).
2. Choose the role **Task Specialist Operator (TSOP)** and the certificate (**ESET annually**, **ESET triennial** or **First Aid**).
3. Click **Add**.

That is all.

> **Careful:** the certificate applies to **all** TSOPs of your company at once, and they all turn red until they have a valid certificate. Have the documents ready first and upload them ([chapter 19](19-bulk-upload.md)) before you add the certificate to the tree.
